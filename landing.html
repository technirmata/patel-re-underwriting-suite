Asset types — the physical property (the what)
SFR · Single Family. A standalone 1-unit house. The most common entry-point asset for new investors.
2–4 unit / Small residential. Duplex, triplex, fourplex. Still residential financing, often house-hackable.
MFR · Multifamily Residential. 5+ residential units. Commercial-side financing (DSCR / agency / Fannie / Freddie SBL).
MHP · Mobile Home Park. Land + lot-rent model. Tenants own the home, you rent the dirt. Recession-resistant cash flow.
Self-storage. Drive-up or climate-controlled units. Low management lift, NOI-driven valuations.
Retail / NNN / Mixed-use / Office / Industrial / Land. Commercial categories. Underwriting hinges on tenant credit, lease term, and cap-rate comps.
Operational modes — what you do with the asset (NOT asset classes)
LTR · Long-Term Rental. Standard 12-month lease. Stable cashflow, lower mgmt lift. Most lenders underwrite this case by default.
MTR · Mid-Term Rental. 30-day to 6-month furnished stays. Travel nurses, corporate housing, insurance-displacement tenants. Higher revenue than LTR; insurance + furnishings add cost.
STR · Short-Term Rental. Nightly Airbnb / VRBO / Booking. Highest revenue ceiling, highest operational + regulatory risk. Many cities restrict.
BRRRR · Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. Value-add playbook on residential. Refi out most/all capital, recycle into next deal.
Fix & flip. Buy distressed, renovate, resell. Profit = ARV − purchase − rehab − holding − selling costs. Capital gains, not rental income.
Wholesale. Contract a property at below-market, assign the contract to an end-buyer for a fee. No capital required to close the assignment.
Value-add / reposition. Buy underperforming, force NOI growth via rent bumps, expense reduction, or repositioning. Typical hold: 3–7 years.
Syndication. Pool LP capital into a GP-led entity to acquire larger deals. Reg D 506(b) or 506(c) offering. GP earns acquisition fee + management fee + promote.
Deal structure — who's on the cap table
GP · General Partner. Deal sponsor / operator. Signs on the loan, executes the plan, earns the promote.
LP · Limited Partner. Passive investor. Writes a check, receives distributions, liability capped at investment.
OM · Offering Memorandum. The marketing PDF brokers send buyers. Always re-underwrite the OM's numbers.
LOI · Letter of Intent. Non-binding offer outlining major terms. Broker etiquette: respond inside 24 hours.
PSA · Purchase & Sale Agreement. Binding contract that supersedes the LOI. Locks price, contingencies, earnest money.
T-12 / T-3 · Trailing 12 / 3 months. Actual financials over the last 12 (or 3) months. The most trustworthy number on the OM.
Return metrics — the numbers you care about
NOI · Net Operating Income. Gross income minus operating expenses (before debt & CapEx). Value = NOI ÷ cap rate.
CoC · Cash-on-Cash. Year-1 pre-tax cash flow ÷ total cash invested. The most-quoted return metric for active investors.
DSCR · Debt-Service Coverage Ratio. NOI ÷ annual debt payments. Lenders want 1.20–1.25× minimum.
IRR · Internal Rate of Return. Time-weighted annualized return across the entire hold, including exit. The institutional standard.
EM · Equity Multiple. Total dollars returned ÷ total dollars invested. 2.0× EM doubled your money.
Promote · GP Carry. The GP's outsized share above the LP pref. Typical institutional MFR: 20–30% of profits above an 8% pref — often the largest line item on a syndication's GP P&L.
Financing — what the lender / banker says
LTV · Loan-to-Value. Loan amount ÷ appraised property value. 65–75% typical for stabilized multifamily.
PITI · Principal, Interest, Taxes, Insurance. The four pieces of a fully-loaded monthly mortgage payment.
ARV · After-Repair Value. What the property will appraise for after renovation. Pivotal in BRRRR and value-add deals.
CapEx · Capital Expenditure. Big-ticket replacements (roof, HVAC, paving). Reserve ≈ $250–$500 / unit / year on stabilized MFR.
DSCR loan. Underwritten on the property's cash flow, not your W-2. 1.20–1.25× DSCR typical floor; up to 80% LTV on stabilized rentals.
Sub-To · Subject-To. Take title while the seller's existing mortgage stays in place. Creative-finance technique — watch out for due-on-sale clauses.

Your stress tests shouldn't be.

A tired analyst at 11 p.m. skips the exit-cap stress test. A junior anchors to the seller's pro forma. A freelance underwriter paid per close has a quiet incentive to find the "yes." Software priced per month doesn't.

😴
Decision fatigue

Analyst forecast accuracy degrades measurably across the trading day. Late-day forecasts default to heuristic shortcuts — herding to consensus, reissuing prior numbers, rounding.

— Hirshleifer, Levi, Lourie & Teoh (2019), Journal of Financial Economics

⚓
Anchoring bias

Underwriters who start from the seller's pro forma consistently arrive at higher valuations than those who build the income statement from raw T-12 / rent-roll inputs.

— CII underwriter guide on cognitive biases

🎯
Confirmation bias

When you love a deal, you stop looking for reasons it might not work. Structured templates run every disconfirming check — exit cap expansion, vacancy spikes, rate shock, refi risk — even when you've already mentally bought.

— Pioneer Publishing on anchoring in financial decisions

💵
Incentive bias

A freelance underwriter paid per closed deal — or an in-house analyst with promote at stake — has skin in the game on the yes side. Patel RE charges $99/mo whether the deal is a yes, a maybe, or a hard no.

— CPI Capital on GP promote economics
